How it works

Three things, in this order.

No feed, no followers, no algorithm. A roll is a private group and the photos its people took.

Start

Open a roll

Name it, give it a cover if you like. That's the whole setup — the first photo becomes the cover otherwise.

Invite

Hold up the code

Every roll has a six-character invite code and a QR. Friends scan it or type it, and they're in. No phone numbers, no contacts access.

Shoot

Everyone shoots into it

Take photos straight from the app or add from your gallery. Everyone else sees them within seconds — even if you lose signal, the upload waits and finishes later.

What's inside

Small on purpose.

Everything a group of friends needs to keep the photos from one week together, and nothing they don't.

Camera
Shoot in, don't upload later. Tap-to-focus, pinch zoom, flash, a self-timer and a rule-of-thirds grid.
Timeline
Grouped by day. Each day opens on its most-starred shot, then the rest.
Stars
Mark the keepers. Filter any roll down to what people starred, or to one person's photos.
Reactions
Five of them. No comment threads, no view counts.
Save & share
Full resolution, yours. Save any photo — or a whole selection — to your gallery, or share it anywhere.
Slideshow
Prop the phone up. It keeps the screen awake and stops at the end.
Offline
Uploads queue. Photos taken in a dead zone go up the moment you're back on a network.
Sign-in
Google, email, or just look around as a guest and save your account later.
Privacy

Private means the people in the roll.

A roll is visible only to its members, and a member is only someone who was handed the invite code. There's no discovery, no public profiles, and nothing you post goes anywhere except to the people you were with.

Every photo is re-encoded on your phone before it leaves. That strips the metadata — GPS coordinates, device model, the original timestamp — so a photo someone saves and forwards carries nothing about where you were standing. The time it was taken is kept separately, inside the roll, where it belongs.

The app never asks to read your photo library. Adding from your gallery uses the system picker, so it only ever sees the photos you choose.

The honest caveat

Photo links inside a roll are long-lived signed URLs. Anyone holding the exact link can open that one image without signing in — the same trade every photo-sharing app makes so images load fast and share cleanly. The links live only inside your roll; they don't leak on their own.
