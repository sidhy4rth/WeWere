The person behind the roll

Sidhyarth

Freshman at Alliance University, Bangalore. Built WeWere in four days in September 2026 — from an empty folder to an app on his friends' phones.

How he works

He decides fast and tests for real. Every build went onto his own phone before it went anywhere else, and the report back was always the plain truth — "it says access denied", "after a few photos it gets stuck" — never a guess about why. Two of the most important bugs in WeWere were found by exactly those sentences.

He asks the question that matters. "Will it keep uploading if I minimise the app?" and "does the upload happen in batches?" are not idle curiosity; they are the questions a user would ask, and asking them early is what turned a demo into something people can rely on.

He is frugal on purpose. When the obvious path meant a card on file and a bill, he walked away from it and the app found a better route. Every service WeWere runs on is on a free tier, and it works.

And he finishes. The instruction at the end of most days was some version of "ship everything right now" — and then he did the part only he could do, hit deploy, and checked it was live.

What he brings

  • Product sense

    Chose the black-and-gold direction, named the app, picked the icon, and kept the scope to one idea done well: a private roll for the people you were with.

  • Shipping with an AI pair

    Took a Kotlin/Compose app with Firebase Auth, Firestore, Supabase Storage and a resumable upload queue from nothing to a signed release — directing the work, not just watching it.

  • Hands-on in the terminal

    Runs the deploys himself: Firebase rules, Vercel, GitHub, adb on a real device. Comfortable being the person who presses the button.

  • Real-device QA

    Installs every build, tries what a user would try, and reports what actually happened. The fastest bug tracker the project has.
